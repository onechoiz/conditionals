Plan A, plan B, plan C

Useful words are highlighted. The conditional verbs are underlined.

Grammar focus — the first conditional

We use it for a real possibility in the future: if + present simple, then will + base verb. If the weather is good, we will walk to the lake.

The order can change, and then there is no comma: We will walk to the lake if the weather is good.

Two things students often miss. Never put will after if — the if part stays in the present. And you can swap will for may, might or could when you are less sure: If it rains, we might stay home.

Unless means if not: Unless it rains, we will walk = If it does not rain, we will walk.

1. A forecast nobody trusted

On Thursday evening the forecast said sun. On Friday morning it said rain. By Friday lunchtime it said both.

Karim, Lena and Sofia had been planning this weekend for a month, and now they were sitting in Lena's kitchen looking at three different weather apps. “If we keep checking,” Sofia said, “we will never decide anything.”

So they did something more useful. Instead of one plan, they made three. “If the morning is dry, we will take the early bus to the lake,” Lena said, writing it down. “If it rains before ten, we will go to the climbing gym instead. And if it rains all day, we will cook something enormous here and watch films.”

Karim laughed, but he agreed. A backup plan is not pessimism, he said. It is just how you stop a weekend from disappearing.

2. Saturday, six forty in the morning

Karim woke up first and looked out of the window. The sky was gray, but nothing was falling. He sent one message to the group: “Dry. Plan A. Unless somebody objects, I will be at the bus stop at seven thirty.”

Nobody objected. They caught the early bus, and by nine they were on the trail that runs along the north side of the lake. It was cool, quiet, and completely empty. Sofia had brought a small stove, Lena had brought too much bread, and Karim had brought a jacket for everybody, “in case the wind changes.”

Around eleven the path became steep, and Lena stopped to look at the map. “There's a shortcut here,” she said. “If we take it, we will save forty minutes. But if the ground is wet, it could be slippery.” They looked at it, tested the first few meters, and chose the long way. Nobody regretted it.

3. What the rain finally did

The rain arrived at four o'clock, exactly when the apps had stopped promising anything. By then they were already walking back toward the village, and it did not matter. They ran the last few hundred meters, laughing, and dried their jackets over the radiator in a small cafe while they waited for the bus.

On the way home Sofia was already talking about the next trip. “If we go again in September,” she said, “we will start even earlier. And if Karim brings that stove again, I might carry the bread.”

They never used plan B or plan C. But having them was the reason nobody spent Friday evening arguing, and the reason they were on a bus at seven thirty instead of still checking their phones. If you plan for the weather you do not want, you will usually get the weekend you do.
